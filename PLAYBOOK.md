**Pick a format (F–K, added 2026-10-07 from the 14-tweet x-batch):** F for a product/UI launch where the *interface* is the hero (six variants, see "when to use which" at the top of Part F); G for a launch film where a strong *art direction* is the hero (serif/navy, frosted glass, 3D devices over photos, flat colour blocks); H for a serious, data-backed announcement (benchmarks, results) with a narrator; I when you want a founder-to-camera feel without filming anyone (generated presenter: disclose it); J for a native-looking UGC ad that ends on the product (sibling of B, ~$0.09); K for a gentle, loopable storytelling post with no voice. # Part A: Cartoon duo

## 1. What the format is

![reference](cartoon-duo/reference-frame.jpg)

Look first: extract a contact sheet from a reference reel (`ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -vf "fps=1/6,scale=240:-1,tile=6x4" -frames:v 1 sheet.jpg`). Then transcribe it with `tools/transcribe_words.py ref.mp3 ref.json --diarize` to understand who says what.

| Element | Spec (canvas 1080×1920, 30 fps) |
|---|---|
| Background | cream `#fefbf2` + dot grid (`radial-gradient` 2px `#ddd6c6`, 33px pitch) |
| Cards | white, `4px solid #111` border, hard shadow `9px 9px 0 #111`, no radius. Accents: yellow `#fdc83c`, pink `#f7a3e6`, green `#a6e38f`, blue `#b8ecf7`, hot pink `#e0418f` for strike-throughs |
| Type | Archivo (variable TTF, bundled locally), 800–900 weight, UPPERCASE, letter-spaced labels |
| "Spine" | A persistent stacked funnel at the top (y≈130–470). One row per step of the script, starting dashed and empty. When its step starts, a row turns solid with a black number box, and the current step is highlighted yellow. This is the backbone of the format. |
| Scene area | y≈500–1050, centered cards that pop in (`back.out`) on the exact spoken word |
| Characters | Speaker bottom-left (short character), listener bottom-right (taller), y≈990–1395, overlapping the scene area a bit |
| Captions | One white box at y=1440, 64px/900, max ~4 words or 16 chars per group, broken at punctuation or >0.35s pauses. The current word gets a yellow background; past words are black, upcoming words gray `#8a8478` |
| Small tags | "ILUSTRATIVO" chip on cards with made-up numbers |

## 2. Prerequisites

## 5. Anatomy of `cartoon-duo/template/index.html`

Order matters inside the `<script>`:

1. **`WORDS`, `END`, `tl`, helpers.** `pop(sel, t)` is a scale/opacity pop-in with `back.out`; `out(sel, t)` fades something away.
2. **Captions.** Built in JS from `WORDS`: groups are split on punctuation, a >0.35s gap, 4 words or 16 chars. Each group is its own box, shown and hidden with `tl.set` opacity. Each word gets a `tl.set` to yellow at its start and drops the yellow just after its end.
3. **Characters.** Everything here is keyed by **word index** (`ws(i)` = start of word i), never by raw seconds, so it survives retiming:
   - `pose(who, name, t)` swaps stacked `<img>` poses with `tl.set` opacity.
   - **Speaker (Jake):** mouth flaps per word (talk at the word start, idle just before its end, an extra blink-close mid-word on long words) plus a small squash bounce per word. On key phrases (`JPOINT` index ranges) he switches to the pointing pose and stretches upward (`scaleY: 1.1`).
   - **Listener (Finn):** reacts at `FREACT` word indices (wow/thumbs-up pose + jump). He nods on the agreeing words and gives a small nod at the start of each new idea.
   - **Both:** idle breathing (`scaleY` yoyo) and sway (`rotation` yoyo), always with a finite `repeat`.
   - **Layering:** each character is nested in 4 wrappers, `#x` > `#x-s` > `#x-x` > `#x-w` > imgs. Separate wrappers mean breathing, sway/stretch/jumps and per-word bounces never tween the same property on the same element.
   - **Mirroring a character** (e.g. to face the other one): use CSS `#finn img { transform: scaleX(-1); }` on the `<img>`s, which are never tweened.
4. **`// ---- scene cues ----`** marker (`retime.py` relies on it). Below it come the spine and the scenes, all in absolute seconds.
5. **Spine.** `activate(i, t)` turns step i on (hides the dashed number, pops the solid row, sweeps the yellow highlight in, retracts the previous one). The finale sweeps every row yellow.
6. **Scenes `S1…S8`.** One `<section class="scene clip">` per beat of the script, each a few cards popping on their trigger words. Ideas we used: strike-through bars (`scaleX` 0→1), counters and countdowns driven by a proxy object `{v}` + `onUpdate` (seek-safe), staggered tiles, a speech bubble aimed at the listener, a cursor clicking a CTA button.

Scene-writing rules of thumb: 1–3 cards visible at a time, every element appears on the word that names it, dim earlier cards (`opacity 0.18–0.4`) instead of removing them when the next idea builds on them, and put "ILUSTRATIVO" on invented stats.

## 7. Gotchas we hit (read before you start)

- **Back up `index.html` before any regex or scripted edit.** CSS and JS share comment names (`/* characters */`, `/* spine */`), so a non-greedy regex between two of them deleted half the file once. Use unique markers and `assert s.count(marker) == 1`.
- HyperFrames `check` errors we fixed:
  - Text overlapping text: a dashed placeholder label under a filled row. Hide the placeholder when the row turns on, or don't put text in placeholders.
  - Contrast below 3:1: gray `#9a9384` on cream fails; `rgb(152,145,130)` passes.
  - Text overflowing its card: widen the card or add `white-space: nowrap`.
- The render log may say `drawElement self-verify failed … re-rendering via screenshot`. That's harmless: it falls back automatically.
- `curl -F file=@…` fails silently on filenames with commas, semicolons or double extensions. Copy the file to a clean path first.
- `treg host` doesn't exist. Generated images already have a public output URL, so reuse it as the reference for pose edits.
- ElevenLabs alignment is per character; group characters into words on whitespace (done in `tts_with_timestamps.py`).
- Keep characters' timing on word indices and scenes on seconds. That split is what makes `retime.py` possible.
- To check a render: `ffprobe` the size and duration, `volumedetect` for audio, and pull frames with `ffmpeg -ss T -i out.mp4 -frames:v 1`. Look at the frames yourself rather than trusting the check alone.

# Part B: Chalkboard explainer

![chalk reference](chalkboard/reference-frame.jpg)

Inspired by the "Chalkboard Explainer" ad format (reference: https://x.com/spwfeijen/status/2107023055532810720, a 23.6s AI video of a hand drawing a "BELLY FAT?" infographic). ## 10. What the format is

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Frame | 1080×1920, 30 fps, ~20–30s. A green chalkboard with an aluminium frame on a light wall fills the frame, shot straight on |
| Board content | A title with quote marks (`¿QUIZ FUNNEL?`), a simple chalk drawing column (left), a numbered list with one small icon per item (right), a short "don't" note with a crossed-out sketch, and a bottom takeaway underlined twice |
| Motion | Elements appear in narration order. Text is written letter by letter, left to right; drawings are traced around their outline. A hand holding chalk follows the drawing point and rests off to the side during pauses, then leaves at the end |
| Audio | Casual, friendly voice (no captions, like the original), a quiet upbeat music bed, and soft chalk scratching only while writing |
| Length rule | One idea per list item, 5 items max. The voice reads the board in drawing order |

## 12. How `render_chalk.py` works

1. **Chalk alpha**: `A = clip((L_final − L_empty − 18) / 45, 0, 1)`, the per-pixel strength of chalk on the finished board. A frame is `empty + (final − empty) × A × reveal(t)`, so the unrevealed board is exactly the empty board, with no seams.
2. **Draw-time map `T[y,x]`**: each element's chalk pixels are split into connected components (letters, strokes).
   - Components are ordered by reading order (text: line band, then x) or top-to-bottom (drawings).
   - Each component gets time in proportion to its ink, with a ~12% pen-lift gap.
   - Inside a component, text uses a diagonal sweep (65% top→bottom, 35% left→right), which reads as handwriting. Drawings sweep by angle around the centroid starting at 12 o'clock, which reads as tracing an outline; thin strokes use their long axis.
   - Each pixel then fades in over 60ms after its time.
3. **Pen path**: while revealing, it samples the centroid of the ink front 2× per frame. Between samples less than 0.5s apart, the hand moves linearly with a ±1.5px tremor. In longer pauses it lifts down-right (+60, +90px), rests, then eases to the next start. It enters from the right before the first stroke and exits bottom-right after the last.
4. **Hand sprite**:
   - The forearm is extended along its axis: rotate the arm vertical, repeat the widest clean row, add the real skin texture from the 120 rows above (opaque pixels only, mirrored tiles), rotate back.
   - The sprite is then rotated `sprite_rotate_deg` and scaled.
   - The chalk tip is found automatically as the top-left-most bright pixel, and the sprite is placed so the tip sits on the pen point.
   - A soft 25% shadow is offset (18, 14)px.
5. Frames are piped as raw RGB into `ffmpeg libx264 crf 17`.

## 13. Gotchas (format B)

- **Short arm**: an arm that ends mid-frame kills the illusion. Use a long-arm image, plus the extension, plus the sprite rotation.
- **Texture seams**: repeating one row looks airbrushed, and copying texture that includes edge pixels gives a sawtooth edge. Use the opaque-only, mirrored texture (already in the script).
- **Rotation sign**: PIL's `rotate(+θ)` is counter-clockwise. To make a down-right arm vertical, rotate by −θ, then rotate back by +θ.
- **NumPy 2** removed `ndarray.ptp()`; use `np.ptp(arr)`.
- Specks on the board frame show up in `suggest`. Leave them out of every box and they're never drawn.
- If a letter looks "wiped" rather than written, the `dur` is too short for that much ink; give it more time, or split the box.
- The voice should not run ahead of the board: start every element on its word, and make the title or hook element finish before the second sentence.

## 18. Techniques (copy-paste reference, all in `brand-film/template/index.html`)

- **Seeded flicker grid**: `rnd()` is a Park–Miller LCG (`seed = seed*16807 % 2147483647`). Each cell gets `tl.set(opacity)` at random-but-deterministic times.
- **Dotted giant text**: `color: transparent; background-image: radial-gradient(circle, rgba(232,56,45,.55) 3px, transparent 3.6px); background-size: 11px 11px; background-clip: text`.
- **Coin burst**: compute each coin's offset to the center (`540 - cx`, `1000 - cy`), then `fromTo({x,y,scale:.3,opacity:0}, {x:0,y:0,scale:1,opacity:1, ease:"expo.out"})` with a 50ms stagger. Float with `tl.to(yPercent ±6, yoyo, finite repeat)`. Use `to`, not a second `fromTo`.
- **Depth of field**: a static `filter: blur(2–5px)` on background coins; when a scene ends, `filter: blur(14px)` on everything for a focus pull.
- **3D card**: parent `perspective: 1600px`; tween the card's `rotationX/Y/Z` and `y` together, then a slow drift tween that starts only after the entry ends.
- **Counters and countdowns**: tween a proxy `{v}` and write `textContent` in `onUpdate` (seek-safe). Use a decimal comma for Spanish (`toFixed(1).replace(".", ",")`).
- **Camera pull-back / zoom-through**: everything lives in `#s5-stage` inside a `perspective` parent. Scale 1.3 (window fills) → 0.88 with `rotationX: 10`, then scale 5 + `blur(18px)` + a radial red flash layer scaling up.
- **Glitch-in**: `fromTo({opacity:0,x:-30,skewX:20,filter:"blur(6px)"}, {… ease:"steps(5)", duration:.22})`, then 4 quick `x: 8` yoyo jitters.
- **Neon draw**: SVG `<text>` with `fill: transparent; stroke: cream; stroke-width: 3`, `strokeDasharray = strokeDashoffset = 2600`, tween the offset to 0 over ~0.9s, plus a `drop-shadow` white+red glow. Echoes: `cloneNode` the SVG 4×, then scale 1→1.25…2 while fading. Then fade the neon out and fade in the solid HTML lockup.

## 19. Gotchas we hit

- **Layout-audit errors that are really design** (`npm run check`):
  - Outline-only giant words fail `text_not_painted`: add `data-layout-ignore`.
  - Collage panels overlap on purpose: add `data-layout-ignore` on each `.panel`, and `data-layout-allow-occlusion data-layout-allow-overlap` on the stage.
  - **A `radial-gradient` vignette counts as an opaque layer** covering text (`text_occluded`). Draw the vignette with `box-shadow: inset 0 0 320px 140px rgba(0,0,0,.7)` instead.
- **Contrast**: brand red `#e8382d` on white is 4.17:1 and fails for small text. Use `#c42a20` for small labels on light cards.
- **Wide stretched type wraps**: Archivo at `font-stretch: 118%`, uppercase, runs ~0.78 em per character. "LO SEGMENTAS," at 130px wrapped, so we use 96px + `white-space: nowrap` and split long lines into two `<div>`s. The same applies to giant background words ("5–8" at 560px wrapped; 420px fits).
- **Starting the camera too zoomed** (scale 1.9) cropped "−30%" off the window; 1.3 works.
- **Lint warnings**:
  - two `fromTo` on the same element → make the second a `to`;
  - overlapping tweens on the same property → end the first before the second starts;
  - an `<audio>` slot longer than the file → set `data-duration` to the real length.
- Generated coins are photoreal and don't need a background removal pass beyond `cutout_grid.py`.

## 25. Gotchas we hit

- **The trace stream ran out**: rows built from 9 repeats scrolled past their own end, leaving the right half empty with ⚠ badges floating in space. Build rows much longer than the distance they travel (we use 22 repeats ≈ 5,000px for ~1,800px of travel).

- **The orb covered the label** on leftward blocks ("Sale" read as "ale"). Fixed with `paddingLeft: 104px` on `dir: "l"` blocks.
- **The countdown showed 15:00 for a second**: start the proxy at `899`, not `899.99` (`Math.ceil`).
- **Dither speckle in the black sky**: clip the dark tones (`floor` 0.16) before dithering, and bias the Bayer thresholds by +0.5/16.
- **Cluster labels hidden under triangles**: place labels at `cy + 175`, outside the max cluster radius (130px × 0.75 vertically).

## 31. Gotchas we hit

- **`cutout_grid.py` dropped the thin stanchion** (its pixel area was under 20% of the biggest object), so every later name shifted by one and the stopwatch was saved as `p_stanchion`. The threshold is now 4% and the script warns on a count mismatch. Always check the montage.
- **Number formatting:** `toLocaleString("es-ES")` prints `1000`, without the dot, for 4-digit numbers. Use `String(n).replace(/\B(?=(\d{3})+(?!\d))/g, ".")` → `1.000`.
- **Text over generated props** needs measuring. Our first sign text landed on the windows, not on the board.
- **The unit grid overlapped the building**: keep data graphics in their own column (grid x 60–580, building x 600+).
- **Labels covering the chart rider**: put the end-value labels above and to the left of the last point; the rider sits at the point.
- **Contrast**: grey notes `#8a8a8f` on paper failed (3.0:1); `#66666b` passes.
- **Big labels near the edge** ("2 de cada 100" at x=700) get cut. Keep `.hl` tags left-aligned at x≈60, or measure their width.

## 32. Before publishing

- Every number on screen in the template is an **example** (1.000 visits, 2/100, 4 → 19 demos, 7 de 10, 2% → 6,8%) and is labelled "ejemplo". Replace it with sourced data, and fact-check as the author did.
- **The style is inspired by Jorge Galindo's video**, with his published frames as the image reference. Style isn't copyrightable, but don't reuse his exact characters or frames. Our characters are newly generated.
- The CTA promises "te enseño cómo montarlo". Have it ready.

## 33. Cost and time

- 2 images (~$0.06), plus ElevenLabs: ~830 characters of TTS, a 58s music track, 5 SFX.
- Render ~52s. A new 1-minute film from the template is ~1–2h of agent time, most of it scene choreography (positions, walks, timings). A longer piece (several minutes, like the original) means more scenes and more iteration on a moodboard and a 20-second test clip first, exactly as the author did.

Shared rules added by batch 2 (on top of the F–K list above):
- **`fromTo` renders its *from* state immediately**, even when the tween starts 20s later. Future windows showed up as thin bars (crt-desktop) and tap ripples were visible from t=0 (mascot-cast). Use `tl.set` at the start time + `to()`, or `immediateRender: false`.
- **Pure-function-of-time canvases** (the particle swarm in N, the cube field in P, the dot-matrix in G3) are the safe way to animate hundreds of elements: compute each frame from `t` and a seeded hash, store nothing between frames.
- **Canvases and the layout auditor:** a full-frame transparent canvas triggers `text_occluded` errors that `data-layout-ignore` does not silence (0.8.137). Verify those frames visually.
- **Audio QA:** run `tools/sfx_normalize.sh` on every SFX set (reused sets too: the airy-launch tick is −50 dB) and `tools/audio_check.sh` on the final mp4 (whole file + first/last 3s). To place cuts, use `tools/music_rms.py`; to fit a bed to the film, use `tools/fit_music.sh`.
- **Copy-paste fonts:** never name a `@font-face` family like a generic one (`"Serif"`). This happened again in Q; the problem only shows up in the mp4.

# SFX that feel AI: avoid them in every format (added 2026-10-08)
**Client rule:** being AI-generated is fine; **sounding AI-made is not**, and one AI-feeling sound makes the whole video feel AI. Professional editors called the Andrés tarot edit "smells AI" because of its sound effects: a riser + impact + boom stack into the title, a `swoosh_in` "chs" on a card entrance, and a whoosh + hit on every teaser cut (62 SFX in 3:49).

**What feels AI** (measured on 80 blind-rated sounds + 8 flagged; see `../sfx-forensics/README.md`):
| Trait | Feels AI | Clean |
|---|---|---|
| Low-end body (<200 Hz) | 0.6% | 6.1% |
| 8 kHz+ "air" (hiss) | 41% | 5% |
| Time held near full level | 34% | 18% |
| Tail | 441 ms | 210 ms |
| Texture | Smooth, no grain | Grainy, transients |
| Shape | Rising sweeps (risers, swooshes) | Hit, then natural decay |
Also: machine-regular repetition (typing loops, chalk_sfx). The worst offenders in this playbook: `crt-desktop-film/…/riser.mp3`, `shimmer.mp3`, `swarm.mp3`, `search-bar-film/…/swoosh_in.mp3`, `color-block-promo/…/swoosh_up.mp3`, `form-dossier-film/…/paper.mp3`, `aura-kinetic-film/…/chime.mp3`, `chalkboard/template/chalk_sfx.mp3`.

**Rules for all formats:**
1. Check every SFX before using it: `tools/sfx_feel_check.sh file.mp3` (model trained on the client's own ratings, ~80% agreement). A score of 0.5 or more means don't use it.
2. Prefer `../sfx-forensics/approved_sounds.txt` (70 sounds that feel clean).
3. No SFX on cuts, card or caption entrances. No stacked riser + impact + boom. At most one accent per title, ideally a beat in the music itself. Only a handful of SFX per video, on real payoffs.
4. When generating new SFX with `el_audio.sh sfx`, ask for short, dry sounds with low-end body (e.g. "short dry wooden thump, close-miked, no reverb"), not "airy whoosh" or "shimmer". Score the result before using it.

## LF1. What all six do (treat as defaults)
1. **~95% hard cuts, on word boundaries, never on the music beat.** No crossfades, circle reveals, whip pans, glitches, light leaks or shakes as transitions in any of the six. The contrast between the face world and the graphic world *is* the transition. Animated transitions are kept for 3–8 structural moments per video (title/chapter cards).
2. **The hook is the densest stretch**: 1.5–2× the body's cut rate (Ali 17 vs 8–15, Iman 34 vs 17–21, HubSpot 22 vs 11–13, Ryan 26 vs 18). Body pacing then **pulses**: explanation stretches slow down (8–13 cuts/min, holds of 10–20 s), montage/proof bursts speed up, emotional or closing parts slow down again.
3. **Graphics density sits at 2.0–3.4/min**, which confirms our ≤ ~3/min cap. Lock Stock (4.0/min, 52% full screens) is the one that feels like slides.
4. **Face framings are static.** Jump cuts change framing (wide ↔ 1.16–1.25× punch-in, or a second angle). Nobody uses a slow push-in inside a shot. Animated zoom ramps are rare (≤0.5/min, 0.46–0.75 s, on the landing line only).
5. **No source processing.** No grade, grain, glow, vignette or light leaks on the footage in any of them. The looks come from lighting on set. This matches our "never process source quality" rule.
6. **SFX are almost absent: 0–0.7 per minute, never on cuts, caption, lower-third or card entrances.** Accents come from the music (a stop, a drop, a new cue on the card). Ali uses one tiny tick per chapter card; Iman reuses one sub-boom only on payoff illustrations (1 in 3 full screens).
7. **A recurring progress device carries the chapters**: Ali's 5-node chapter map, Iman's 7-sector dial, Ryan's tier badge and "room type" card, Lock Stock's typewriter titles, HubSpot's time-of-day stamps. The viewer always knows "3 of 7".
8. **Recurring, growing graphics**: one diagram, timeline or icon cast comes back 4–20 times and adds one element per visit (Ali's whiteboard, HubSpot's timeline and team diagram, Lock Stock's government/bank/investor icons, Ryan's page counter). One evolving graphic replaces 5 separate ones and costs little against the graphics budget.
9. **Music: one continuous bed, changed (or stopped) at chapter boundaries, arranged against the speech.** The bed is louder in the hook and under no-voice montages and graphics, and it drops out for sponsor reads, interviews and "serious" lines.
10. **Ends on the face**, a one-line takeaway + verbal CTA, no outro swell.

## LF4. Music and SFX recipes
1. **Bed level:** body 10–16 dB under the voice, hook 3–6 dB louder than the body (never closer than ~6 dB to the voice on dense Spanish talk), **+6 to +8 dB under no-voice graphics and montages** (Iman 7P swells ~6 dB under each illustration; Ryan's montages put music in front). Sidechain ducking 8–10 dB, attack 20–150 ms, release 400 ms. Master −14 LUFS / −1.5 dBTP.
2. **Chapter landing:** a music micro-stop of 0.2–0.35 s (−30 dB, 30 ms fades) right before each chapter card, then the new cue (or the next section of the track) starts on the card's first frame (Iman 7P). Or leave a 0.8–1.0 s voice gap at the card and raise the bed +3 dB under it (Ali).
3. **Bass-out → drop on the payoff:** high-pass the bed (`highpass=f=180`) for 5–25 s under a set-up and release it on the reveal word with a 50 ms ramp. Optional 0.25–0.3 s full stop just before the payoff word. 3–6 times per long video, only on beds with a real bass line (Iman AI).
4. **Music out for the serious read:** fade out 0.3 s ending 0.5 s before a sponsor read, disclaimer or core thesis; the read runs dry; a new cue hits on the first frame after it. ≤2 per video, dry stretch ≤2 min (Ryan).
5. **Track changes** every 2–3 min, near chapter boundaries, never mid-sentence. No beat-sync of cuts in talk sections; a beat-cut list (~2 beats per item) is OK in the hook only.
6. **SFX budget:** ≤1 accent per chapter card (a dry tick or pop, not a rising "bloop") and ≤1 short dry low thump (≤0.8 s, not Iman's 2.4 s boom) per ~2 min on payoff illustrations. Nothing on cuts, captions, pills, lower thirds, counters, typewriter titles or pushes. Score each with `tools/sfx_feel_check.sh` (<0.5).

## LF5. Don't copy
- **Their densities:** Lock Stock's 52% and Iman AI's 57% full screens (slides with a talking head), 42 s static text holds, Iman 7P's 34 cuts/min hook with 5 full screens in minute 1, HubSpot's 14 logo stickers in 64 s, Ryan's 18–29 cuts/min vlog pacing.
- **Stock-asset grab-bags:** Freepik/Storyset characters, stock character animations, ticker-board clichés, raw uncropped screenshots with site UI, third-party photos (rights risk). Use one brand world and our own sticker/icon cast.
- **AI-feeling sounds they do use:** Ali's rising "bloop" on title cards, Iman's 2.4 s sub-boom tail and machine-regular dial ticks, HubSpot's per-letter pitched blips.
- **Faked practical looks:** don't fake hands on paper, "archival" footage, neon set lighting or a moody grade on client footage. That is source processing and reads as AI.
- **Channel-specific chrome:** fake subscribe-button animations, always-on watermark bugs, chrome 3D extruded money text, emoji captions, plasma rings, another brand's palette (Ali's pastels, Clay's claymorphism). Keep the *system* (neutral canvas + 4–5 node colours + 1 emphasis colour) in each client's own palette.
- **Stale chapters:** regenerate YouTube chapters from the final cut's chapter-card times (Iman 7P's are ~40 s off).

## LF6. 1080p re-measurement: what changed and where the tokens are (2026-10-09)
**What 360p got systematically wrong** (apply to any future teardown measured at low resolution):
1. **Text was measured about 1.5–2× too small.** Real sizes are large: Ryan's card title 232 px, HubSpot's kinetic words 148 px, Lock Stock's concept words 215 px, Ali's typed text 92 px. Long-form text on a 1080p frame is big and short.
2. **Colours read desaturated.** Chroma subsampling greyed out every accent (Ali's cyan is `#65CDF0`, not `#74CBE1`; Lock Stock's yellows and greens are near-pure `#fbfd00` / `#14ff12`). Iman 7P's "dark purple" cards are pure `#000000`.
3. **Corner radii were measured 2–4× too small**: Ali's PiP r 40 and panel r 50, Ryan's web card r 105, HubSpot's tiles r 57 and cards r 48–60.
4. **Shadows and glows were invented.** Most elements have **no drop shadow** (Ali's PiP, pills and face icons; Iman AI's paper; HubSpot's kinetic text; Ryan's spotlight has no white glow). Where light is used it is a same-colour glow (Iman AI captions) or a tight hard shadow (HubSpot's title card, vlog subtitles).
5. **Overshoot was invented.** Measured frame by frame, entrances ease out with **no overshoot** (Iman 7P card, Iman AI logos, Lock Stock rising letters and flag). Only Ali's pills/tiles have a small 1.09 peak.
6. **Several "animations" were cuts or occlusion.** Ryan's split cells and doc re-entry, Iman AI's page reframes and "logo fade-in" (a hand passing over the page) are hard cuts.

**New details worth copying:**
- **Motion on twos** (Iman 7P): every graphic animates at 12 fps over 24 fps footage, which is a large part of its hand-made feel. In HyperFrames, seek the graphics timeline at `Math.floor(t*12)/12`. Use it per video, not mixed.
- **Ghost-word captions** (Iman AI): lay out the whole phrase at 52% opacity, then switch each word solid on its word time. It reads calmer than words popping in from nothing.
- **Motion-tracked lower thirds** on handheld footage (HubSpot) instead of screen-fixed ones.
- **Font IDs** (letterform-based, "likely"): Ali = Recoleta-like serif + SF Pro; Lock Stock = American Typewriter Bold + Futura Condensed ExtraBold + Futura; Iman 7P = Montserrat (Bold + Regular pairs in one label); Iman AI = SF Pro Display Bold captions, Manrope pages, Monument Extended titles; HubSpot = Helvetica Neue + Druk Condensed; Ryan = Arial only. Each uses 1–3 families.
